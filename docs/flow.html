The map, the links and the strip

How a summon is decided, what the map does while it is up, and how the strip lives and dies. Drawn by hand from the code as of October 2026; the file and function under each diagram is where to check it.

Two surfaces, two rules. The map follows AeroSpace: the ring is AeroSpace's focus and no key walks it. The strip is a picker: a marking is a choice in the making, and keys move it until Enter makes it AeroSpace's focus. One truth about focus runs through both: AeroSpace's focus-changed event, applied the moment it lands.

1. A link arrives

Every summon is a URL AeroSpace opens: aerocontrol://workspaces for the map, aerocontrol://windows for the app you are in, aerocontrol://windows?app=<bundle id> for one app's key. Three of the four cases are settled from AeroSpace's state alone, at once and without a word; only the fourth needs you, and that is the strip.

2. The map, from summon to pictures

The overview is a one shot. It reads everything once, shows the layout at once, and lets the pictures land in it. While it is up it follows AeroSpace's events, and it is gone the moment you focus something.

Opening

flowchart TD
  S["summon"] --> R["reload: list-windows with layout rects, list-workspaces, the focus"]
  R --> D["summonApp decides (section 1)"]
  D -- "the map, or a strip" --> M["measure every window's size from the window server"]
  M --> F["follow AeroSpace's events"]
  F --> W["build the window · reveal after 120 ms"]
  W --> P["take the pictures · each card lands whole, 25 ms apart"]
  D -- "focus, toggle, start" --> X["nothing appears · the shot is done"]

While it is up

3. The strip, from opening to closing

The strip is a value (Strip, in Common): whose windows, the marking, where the carousel centres, how many turns it has taken. Every change is a new value by one of five transitions. The store holds the current one and nothing else.

Under the cards a legend lists the windows, one line each: key, title, workspace when the app spans several, the marked line in the accent. The cards are true to AeroSpace's geometry; the legend is readable whatever that geometry is.

Sources/Common/Domain/Strip.swift (the value and its transitions), AppStripModel.action and start (the keys and the opening rule), OverviewStore.handleStrip, stepStrip, markStrip, pointStrip; the following and the kept transitions are applied in OverviewStore.send and the model's didSet.

4. One way out

Whatever the surface, the shot ends in one place: the store sends a focus action and says onShotDone, or the strip is over and says it too. The host hides the window, and brings the focused app forward when the focus did not just move there.

flowchart LR
  T["click a tile, Enter, ⌘-digit, a card, a badge, an app's key"] --> FA["send(.action(.focusWindow / .focusWorkspace))"]
  FA --> CMD["aerospace focus / workspace"]
  FA --> DONE["onShotDone(restoreFocus: false)"]
  LEAVE["the strip is over: focus left to an app of one window, or its key in that app"] --> DONE2["onShotDone(restoreFocus: true)"]
  ESC["Escape · ⌘W · backdrop · the link again"] --> HIDE["hide(restoreFocus: true)"]
  DONE --> HIDE2["hide"]
  DONE2 --> HIDE

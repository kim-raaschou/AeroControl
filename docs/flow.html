4. The map

Hand-written, from OverlayWindowManager.show: read everything once (reload: list-windows with layout rects, list-workspaces, the focus), decide the summon (section 1), measure every window's size, follow AeroSpace's events, build the window and reveal it after 120 ms, then take the pictures, which land card by card. The arrows, ⌘ and an arrow, and pointing move the ring on the map as they move the marking in the strip (Strip.moved, GridWalk): until a key or the pointer moves it the ring is AeroSpace's focus, and AeroSpace's focus stays on screen as the thin outline. Tab, Home, End and the function keys do nothing.

⇧⌘ and a workspace's name (FilterKey.moveToWorkspace, 0 the tenth) moves the window under the ring to that workspace, on the map and in the strip, and the overview stays up. Two ⌘ keys act on the window under the ring, as they would without the overview, taken before the filter sees them (OverviewStore.commandTarget, OverviewWindow.performKeyEquivalent): ⌘W closes it, as its × does, and ⌘Q quits its app, on the map and in the strip, and the overview stays up; with no window under the ring ⌘W closes the overview. Every other ⌘ key does nothing.

5. One way out

Hand-written, from OverviewStore.send and OverlayWindowManager: a focus action (focus window, focus workspace) makes the store say onShotDone(restoreFocus: false), and a strip that is over (following returned nil) makes it say onShotDone(restoreFocus: true). The host hides the window either way, bringing the focused app forward when the focus did not just move there. Escape and a click on the backdrop hide it directly.
